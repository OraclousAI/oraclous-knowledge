# Documentation Conventions

How we write, organise, and maintain documentation across the Oraclous knowledge base.

## Writing principles

- **Prose, not bullets, by default** — bullets where the content is genuinely a list
- **Short paragraphs** — three to six sentences is the sweet spot
- **No marketing voice** — describe what is true, with its limitations and trade-offs
- **Concrete over abstract** — examples beat principles when both are possible
- **Cross-reference, don't duplicate** — link to the source-of-truth page
- **British English** — "behaviour," "organisation," "favour"

## Status conventions

- `placeholder` — structure fixed; content not yet substantive
- `draft` — content present, not yet reviewed
- `current` — reviewed, in force
- `locked` — content declared LOCKED at a known version
- `deprecated` — superseded; kept for historical reference

## Page anatomy

Each substantive page should have: a descriptive title, a one-paragraph opener, a status block where relevant, headings with semantic meaning, and a Related References block at the bottom.
